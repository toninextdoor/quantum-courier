# Room 905 — cover and demonstration

## Gemini cover prompt

Create original promotional cover artwork for an indie cooperative browser game called ROOM 905. Landscape 16:9, 1920×1080. Set it in a fictional after-hours lakeshore hotel inspired by Mississauga and the Greater Toronto Area: distant lake lights, restrained modern hotel architecture, brass room-number plaque, deep navy, teal holograms and warm amber lighting. Foreground: a small recovery trolley stacked with delicate hotel valuables, a ceramic vase and framed artwork. Two stylized recovery crew members coordinate in a softly lit corridor; a third Guardian watches an original slim biped service robot with a small teal sensor. Through a separate composition panel or reflection, show a Navigator at a translucent three-dimensional mission-control map. Suggest communication and asymmetric teamwork. Atmospheric and intriguing, clean silhouettes, professional game-key-art composition, readable at thumbnail size. No combat, weapons, blood, injuries, theft imagery, real people, celebrity likenesses, owls, music-label symbols, recognizable existing game characters or third-party brands. Do not imitate OVO, R.E.P.O. or another artist's signature style. Leave uncluttered space in the upper-left for the title to be added manually. Do not generate text, logos, fake awards or interface screenshots. This is concept artwork, not a screenshot of gameplay.

Add the exact title ROOM 905 yourself in a clean licensed/system font after generation; check the challenge uploader's image requirements before exporting. Keep any supplied emblem separate unless you have verified rights to use it.

## Strongest companion material

Use actual gameplay captures rather than generated images for feature proof. A 30–45 second clip can show: two devices join the same code; Navigator spots a hidden Flux Fault and sends a warning; Recovery follows the route with fragile cargo; Guardian pauses the Sentinel; trolley reaches quota and the team result appears. Record an ordinary round without developer advantages. Add brief captions for the roles and browser/device support. Do not claim it supports a particular concurrent player count unless tested on the deployed plan.

Only the cover needs generated artwork right now. The existing code-drawn hotel art and game visuals do not require replacement. Avoid spending the remaining polish time adding unrelated features.
